# SLOW BLINK: concept + outline v1

Long form, 16:9 (1920×1080, the NIGHT ROAD kit: `src/film/nr-kit.tsx`), second person, present tense.
Target 9–10 min, ~1,150–1,250 words at ~125 wpm. Narrator: the user's voice (Chatterbox).

**Title:** If Your Cat Stands Up at Night, Close Your Eyes
Alternates: *If Your Cat Slow-Blinks at You at 3 AM, Don't Look Where It's Looking* · *Never Stare Longer Than Your Cat*

**Thumbnail:** dark bedroom seen from the pillow. At the foot of the bed an orange cat stands up on its back legs, its
back to us, facing the open door. Two green eyeshine dots: the cat's, looking back over its shoulder at us.
In the black of the hallway, much higher up, a second pair, very faint. Text: **IF YOUR CAT DOES THIS… / CLOSE YOUR EYES.**

---

## The idea in one line

Your cat isn't the scary thing. It's the only one in the apartment that can see the scary thing, and it has been
trying to tell you, in cat language, what to do.

## The true fact that flips (what makes it shareable)

- In cat body language a **hard, direct stare is a challenge** and a threat. A **slow blink** means "I'm not a threat"
  (University of Edinburgh feline body-language guide; Humphrey et al. 2020, *Scientific Reports*). Cat owners already
  know this. Everybody has heard "slow-blink at your cat, it means I love you".
- **The flip:** the slow blink isn't only affection. It's the cat telling you to **back down**: *close your eyes, stop
  staring, I'll keep watch.* Because the thing in the hallway **speaks cat too**. Staring at it is a challenge, and it
  answers challenges by coming closer.
- Supporting real facts, one line each, used as beat chips:
  - Cats hear far above us (up to ~64–85 kHz vs ~20 kHz). *When your cat stares at nothing, it's hearing something.*
  - Cats' eyes **shine** in the dark (tapetum lucidum). **Human eyes don't.** So if a light catches two shining eyes at
    the height of a person's face…
  - A frightened cat puffs its tail and makes itself **bigger**. Standing up is the biggest it can get.

What the viewer takes away: *if your cat slow-blinks at you at night, close your eyes.* It's harmless (and a real
bonding tip), so people will try it on their own cats tonight and send the video to a friend who has a cat.

## Rule of the threat (never said outright)

**It comes closer to whoever stares at it.** It can only be seen properly in the dark, from the corner of your eye, or
as eyeshine. A slow blink, or closing your eyes, ends the challenge and it backs off. The cat can see it all the time.

**Engine:** the cat's behaviour is the warning system. Each strange thing it does has a meaning, and the story teaches
them one by one: staring down the hall → sitting on your chest → puffed tail → hissing → the slow blink → **standing up**.
**Meter (top right):** **STARE**. It rises while your eyes are open and pointed into the dark, and drains when you
look away or close them. On screen it's a stepped eye icon that opens wider.
**HUD:** `SLOW BLINK · NIGHT n · clock · APT 1B`, chip for the cat's state (`TOAST · CALM / WATCHING / PUFFED / STANDING`).
**Big question:** what happened to Bean, the last tenant's cat? It's answered by the last scene.

## Characters

- **You.** You just moved into a cheap ground-floor apartment, 1B, with your cat of six years.
- **Toast.** Orange tabby, chunky, a white chest. He's the signature colour: orange reads in every dark pixel frame.
  He has never once stood on two legs.
- **Ruth.** The last tenant (never seen). She left in a hurry two years ago, along with an empty bowl that says **BEAN**,
  a cat flap screwed shut, and a stack of index cards.
- **Bean.** Ruth's black cat. Gone the same night as Ruth.
- **Mr. Alvarez, 1A.** The neighbour across the hall. One scene, one fact.
- **The thing in the hallway.** Never named or explained. Tall, pale, a head that tilts around the door frame
  (`tall_one`, `peer` frame). Mostly eyeshine and shape.

## Outline (timecodes at ~125 wpm)

### COLD OPEN (0:00–0:45)
1. Your cat has never stood up on two legs. Not once in six years.
2. Tonight, at three twelve, Toast is standing at the foot of your bed.
3. Up on his back legs. His back to you. Facing the open door.
4. Then he turns his head, looks right at you, and blinks. Slowly.
5. You know what that means. Everybody knows. It means *I love you.*
6. That's not all it means.

> Bed POV, Toast rising onto his hind legs in three held drawings; eyeshine as he looks back; the slow blink is a
> half-blink, half-blink, close, played in held frames.
> TITLE: SLOW BLINK / 3:12 AM. HOOK HEADLINE: *IF YOUR CAT DOES THIS… / CLOSE YOUR EYES.*

### 1. APARTMENT 1B (0:45–2:15)
- Moving day. The rent is too low. The landlord says the last tenant "left in a hurry, kept to herself".
- Things she left: a bowl with **BEAN** painted on it, and a cat flap in the back door, screwed shut from the inside.
- Toast won't walk down the hallway. He sits on the bedroom threshold and stares at the dark end of it.
- Fact chip: *cats hear up to 4× higher than you.* "When your cat stares at nothing, it's hearing something you can't."
- **Cliff:** that night you look down the hall. The dark at the end is a different shape from the one it was at dinner.

### 2. THE CHEST (2:15–3:45)
- Night 2. You wake up unable to breathe. Toast is sitting on your chest, heavy, facing the door, his tail puffed to twice its size.
- You look where he's looking. The longer you look, the more there is. The **STARE** meter appears and climbs.
- A floorboard creaks in the hall. Closer. It creaks again every time you keep looking.
- Toast hisses. Not at the door. **At you.** Then he slow-blinks.
- You're too tired to argue. Your eyes close. The creaking stops.
- **Cliff:** morning. Scratches on the *outside* of your bedroom door. Not at cat height. At yours.

### 3. RUTH'S CARDS (3:45–5:30)
- The screwed-shut cat flap. You unscrew it. Taped inside it is a stack of index cards in Ruth's handwriting:
  *"For whoever gets 1B. If you have a cat, read these. If you don't, get one."*
- The rules, one card at a time (rule cards, each right after the object or behaviour it's about):
  1. **If your cat stares down the hall, look at your cat. Not the hall.**
  2. **Never stare into the dark longer than your cat does.**
  3. **If your cat slow-blinks at you, close your eyes. Keep them closed until you feel your cat lie down.**
  4. **If your cat stands up, don't move. He's making himself bigger than you.**
  5. **Bean is gone. If you see a black cat, it isn't Bean.**
- The flip, on the back of the last card: *"A stare is a challenge. A slow blink means 'I give up'. It speaks cat."*
- **Cliff:** you look up from the cards. Toast is in the hallway doorway, staring past you, into the kitchen.

### 4. NIGHT THREE (5:30–7:30): the set piece
- Storm, power out. Only your phone light.
- Toast is on the end of the bed. He stares at the door. You follow rule 1: you look at Toast, not the door.
- But you need to know. You sweep the phone light across the doorway.
- Fact chip: *cats' eyes shine in the dark. Human eyes don't.*
- Two shining eyes at cat height: Toast's reflection in the wardrobe mirror. Then two more. **Six feet up.**
- The STARE meter goes red. Toast **stands up**, the first full stand-up in the story, on the word *stands*.
  Rule 4: you don't move.
- Toast turns his head and slow-blinks. You close your eyes.
- **Eyes-closed sequence (20–30 s):** black screen, captions and sound only. A creak at the door. One at the foot of
  the bed. A breath that isn't yours, close. Toast's growl, very low, then a hiss and a scuffle. Silence. Then a weight
  on the bed: Toast lying down against your leg, purring. Rule 3: now you can open your eyes.
- **Cliff:** morning. Toast is fine. Except the tip of his left ear is torn.

### 5. BEAN (7:30–9:00)
- Afternoon. A black cat sits outside your window. It doesn't blink. Toast won't go near the glass.
- In the hall, Mr. Alvarez from 1A: "Ruth's cat? Bean went missing the same night Ruth did. Two years ago this week."
- Rule 5. You close the blinds.
- Night 4. You follow every card. Toast stares down the hall; you look at Toast. Toast slow-blinks; you close your eyes.
- **Eyes-closed sequence 2:** Toast's hiss, far off: he's at the door, on the other side of the room, holding it.
  Then soft paws on the bed. Something lies down across your legs. It purrs.
  The purr is too slow, like someone saying the word *purr*.
- Rule 3: keep your eyes closed until you feel your cat lie down.
- You just felt a cat lie down. And you can hear yours, at the door, still hissing.

### ENDING (9:00–9:40)
- "What do you do?"
- **NumberedChoice:** *1: OPEN YOUR EYES* (green, help Toast) / *2: KEEP THEM CLOSED* (red, follow the rules). Both cost
  something: open them and you're staring at whatever is on your legs; keep them closed and Toast fights alone.
- "Comment one or two. Your comments decide what happens on night five."
- **End card:** SLOW BLINK / NIGHT 5 / 3:12 AM / FOLLOW FOR NIGHT 5 / YOUR COMMENTS DECIDE WHAT HAPPENS.

## How it follows the blueprint

| Blueprint rule | Here |
|---|---|
| Hook headline on frame 0, frame 0 is the cover | Cat standing up at the foot of the bed + *IF YOUR CAT DOES THIS… / CLOSE YOUR EYES* |
| One true everyday fact that flips | The slow blink / a stare is a challenge |
| A new question about every 10 s, a cliffhanger every ~90 s | One cliff at the end of each chapter |
| Object → rule card | Each card lands right after the behaviour it explains (the hall stare, the chest, the stand-up) |
| Offscreen proof by sound | The eyes-closed sequences: the fight is only heard |
| Threat seen after 60%, through something, one move | Eyeshine six feet up at ~62%, in a phone light sweep; the head tilt in the door frame, once |
| One full dropout | The silence after the scuffle in night three |
| Innocent in the dilemma, both choices cost | Toast |
| Signature colour | Orange Toast |

## Production notes (kit reuse vs new)

**Reuse:** `nr-kit.tsx` (16:9 world, `CaptionL`, `HudL`, `SeenMeter` → fork to STARE, `RuleCardL`, `TitleL`,
`QuestionL`, `PhoneL`), `tall_one` (`peer`, `front`), `Glow` for eyeshine and the phone light, `LightMask` for the phone
torch, reels-ui `NumberedChoice` / `NextDayCard`, Chatterbox + friend/janitor-style fx for Mr. Alvarez.

**New assets (code or ChatGPT via `prompts/asset-prompts.md`):**
- **Cat atlas** (`toast`, plus `bean` as a black recolour, `toast_torn` ear variant): sit, loaf, walk0–3, stare, chest-sit
  (from the bed POV), tail puff / arch, hiss, slow-blink stages (open → half → half → closed → open), **stand0–2**
  (sitting → rearing → fully upright, back view and 3/4 look-back), lie-down / curled. Eyes are flat pixels;
  the shine is added in Remotion so it can flash in the phone light.
- **Sets (240×135):** the bed POV looking down the duvet to the door and hall (the recurring establishing shot that
  returns each night with new information); the hallway; the kitchen with the back door and cat flap; the window
  (day, black cat outside); the 1A doorway.
- **Inserts:** the index cards (UI component, handwritten font or pixel hand-lettering), the BEAN bowl close-up, the
  cat-flap screws.

**New effects:**
- `Eyelids`: smooth top and bottom lids closing over the frame, plus a held black for the eyes-closed sequences
  (captions stay up).
- Eyeshine pairs: two tiny `Glow`s that only light inside the phone torch cone.

**New sound:** `purr` (a low ~25 Hz rumble with a slow amplitude flutter) plus a **wrong purr** for the end (slowed,
voiced); `hiss`; `growl`; `creak`; a scuffle. The eyes-closed sequences are carried by sound alone, so they need a
real listen before delivery.
