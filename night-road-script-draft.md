# NIGHT ROAD: script draft v1

Long form, 16:9 (1920×1080), second person, present tense. Target 7–8 min.
Title: **If You're Driving Alone at Night, Never Turn On the Inside Light**
Thumbnail: lit cabin, hero's face, the black windows turned to mirrors with pale faces in them. Text: NEVER TURN THIS ON.

**Rule of the threat (never said outright):** they stand in the dark and can't see into a dark car.
A lit car is a shop window. Every light near you is a risk: the inside light, the phone screen, the brake lights, the door.
**Engine:** you can only read Dad's card when there's light, and light is the thing you can't have. The rules come out one at a time, by whatever light breaks through.
**Big question:** what was on the torn-off rule 7? It's answered at the end.
**Meter:** SEEN (top right). It rises with any light on or near you and falls back slowly.
**HUD:** NIGHT ROAD · clock · ROUTE 9 · MILE n · FUEL chip.

Voices: narrator = you (user). Dad and the radio host/caller = the user's voice with FX, like AFTER HOURS.

---

## COLD OPEN (0:00–0:35)

1. Your dad always said it was illegal to turn on the light inside the car at night.
2. It isn't. You looked it up.
3. He just never told you the real reason.
4. Tonight, you find his list.

> Thumb on the dome switch → hard cut to black → the window as a mirror with a face behind yours (8 frames).
> TITLE: NIGHT ROAD / 11:40 PM

## 1. THE DETOUR (0:35–2:00)

5. It's eleven forty. You're driving his old car home for winter break.
6. Two hours to go. Half a tank. Phone at thirty percent.
7. Then the highway on your map turns red. A crash, both lanes.
8. Your phone reroutes you onto Route 9. Forty miles of farmland. No towns.
9. At the first mile marker, the streetlights stop.
10. Your headlights are the only light for miles.
11. A truck comes the other way with its high beams on. You flip the sun visor down.
12. A folded card drops into your lap.
13. Dad's handwriting. NIGHT ROAD. Seven rules.
14. The bottom is torn off. You'll only ever read six.
15. The truck's lights are still on the card, so you read fast.
16. Rule number one. Never turn on the inside light. Not for a second.
17. Rule number two. Don't stop for anyone standing by a mile marker. They aren't hitchhiking.
18. Rule number three. If the radio changes station by itself, leave it. Don't turn it off.
19. The truck passes. It's too dark to read the rest.
20. And to read the rest, you'd need a light.

> RULE #1 card lands on 16 (sub: NOT FOR A SECOND.). RULE #2 and #3 only listed on the card, with no big cards yet. Each one gets its card when it's tested.

## 2. THE MARKERS (2:00–3:10)

21. Mile marker twelve. Someone is standing next to it.
22. A pale shirt. Arms at its sides. Facing the road.
23. Your headlights hit its face, and it doesn't squint.
24. In the mirror, it's still facing the spot where you were.
25. Mile marker thirteen. Someone is standing next to it.
26. Same shirt. One step closer to the road.

> RULE #2 card (sub: THEY AREN'T HITCHHIKING.)

27. Up ahead, red taillights. Another car. You're not alone out here.
28. It's slow. The driver keeps leaning across the seat, looking for something.
29. Then the inside of that car lights up.
30. You can see the driver clearly, like someone in a shop window.
31. Along the road, every figure turns its head.
32. The car pulls onto the shoulder. Its hazards blink twice. Then nothing.
33. You go around it. You don't look.
34. Mile marker twenty. There's a car on the shoulder ahead.
35. The same car. Doors open. Inside light on. Nobody in it.
36. Handprints all over the outside of the glass.

> SEEN meter introduced on 29 (it reads the other car first, then you).

## 3. THE PHONE AND THE RADIO (3:10–4:20)

37. Your phone lights up on the passenger seat. A text.
38. The whole car goes blue.
39. And by that light, you can read rule four.
40. Keep your phone face down. Screens count.
41. Out in the field, something stands up.
42. You slap the phone over. Dark again.

> RULE #4 card (sub: SCREENS COUNT.). The meter spikes to red, then falls.

43. Then the radio clicks.
44. Your music cuts out, and a station you've never heard comes in.
45. A call-in show.
    - (radio, host) Caller, you're on the air. Where are you?
    - (radio, caller) Route nine. Behind a gray car with one taillight out.
46. Your taillight has been out since July.
47. You reach for the dial. Rule three. You leave it.
48. There are no headlights behind you.
    - (radio, caller) It's slowing down.
49. You aren't.

> RULE #3 card on 47 (sub: DON'T TURN IT OFF.)

## 4. DAD (4:20–4:55), the midpoint

50. Your phone buzzes against the seat. It doesn't stop. Dad.
51. You answer without turning it over.
    - (dad, phone) Where are you?
52. Route nine.
53. He doesn't say anything for a long time.
    - (dad, phone) Did you find the card?
54. You did.
    - (dad, phone) Whatever you hear, don't turn on the light. Not even if it's me.
55. The call drops. No signal.

## 5. THE GAS STATION (4:55–6:10), the big moment at ~68%

56. The fuel light comes on with a chime.
57. Eighteen miles left in the tank. The next town is twenty-six.
58. You hold the card up to the dashboard glow.
59. Rule five. If you have to stop, stop under a light. Never stop in the dark.
60. Rule six. If something lands on the roof, don't brake.
61. On a hill ahead, a gas station. Every light on. Nobody at the register.
62. You pull in under the brightest light and kill the engine.
63. Past the edge of the canopy, the lot is black.
64. They're standing in a line right where the dark begins. Toes on the edge of the light.
65. They don't come any closer. They wait.

> RULE #5 card on the pull-in (sub: NEVER IN THE DARK.)

66. You pump with your back to them. Ten dollars. Twenty. Thirty.
67. Above you, one of the canopy lights starts to buzz.
68. It goes out. The line takes one step forward.
69. You hang up the nozzle, open the door, and get in.
70. And the inside light comes on. Doors do that.
71. You slam it shut. The light doesn't go off. It fades, the way it always does.
72. Ten seconds.
73. In the windows, all you can see is yourself.
74. And behind your reflection, faces. Every window.
75. Three. Two. One.
76. Dark.

> The meter maxes out. The HUD counts down 10 → 0 in red. The windows-as-mirrors shot is the thumbnail frame.

## 6. THE BACK SEAT (6:10–7:15)

77. You pull out fast.
78. Something lands on the roof.
79. Your foot moves toward the brake. Rule six. You don't.
80. It slides down the back window and it's gone.

> RULE #6 card (sub: BRAKE LIGHTS COUNT.)

81. Mile marker thirty. Nobody standing by it.
82. Mile marker thirty-one. Nobody.
83. The radio comes back.
    - (radio, host) Caller, are you still there?
    - (radio, caller) I'm in the car now.
84. Then, from the back seat, someone clears their throat.
    - (dad, back seat) Kiddo. It's me.
85. It's his voice. The way he says it.
    - (dad, back seat) I dropped my glasses back here. Turn the light on for a second.
86. Your hand goes up to the switch.
87. Not even if it's me.
88. You put your hand back on the wheel.
    - (dad, back seat) Kiddo. The light.
89. It keeps asking. Then it stops asking.
90. Mile marker forty. A streetlight. Then another.
91. The light sweeps across the visor, and you see a strip of paper tucked behind it.
92. The torn-off bottom of the card.
93. Rule seven. I will never ask you to turn on the light.
94. In the mirror, under the streetlights, the back seat is empty.

## ENDING (7:15–7:45)

95. Two oh eight AM. Dad's on the porch.
96. He doesn't hug you. He walks around the car first.
97. He looks into the back seat for a long time.
    - (dad) Did you turn it on?
98. No.
    - (dad) Good.
99. He takes the keys out of your hand and locks the car.
100. Behind him, inside the car, the light turns on by itself.
101. Would you have turned it on? Comment below.

> End card: NIGHT ROAD / 2:08 AM. The light turning on is the last moving image.
